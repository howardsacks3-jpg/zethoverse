05 / Response protocol

What happens when a threat is detected?

RED ALERT · Critical

Examples: unexpected minting, authority or program upgrade, suspicious treasury movement, vesting permission change or a confirmed compromise indicator. Planned response: immediate founder email, evidence links, affected assets and a step-by-step containment checklist.

WARNING · Investigate

Examples: unusual sales or volume, unexpected wallet activity, unexplained balance changes or missed monitoring checks. Planned response: investigate, verify independently and escalate if confirmed.

INFORMATION · Routine

Examples: normal verified transactions, successful checks and scheduled security summaries. Planned response: transparent public record and periodic report.

Security reality: ZETHOSPHERE is being designed to reduce risk through monitoring, early warning and a rehearsed response. It cannot guarantee that hacking will be prevented or reverse a finalized Solana transaction. Alerts must be verified before irreversible action.
06 / Emergency readiness

Founder emergency response playbook

Defensive, non-destructive guidance for suspected attacks, unauthorized activity and service outages. Public visitors see this safety overview; sensitive investigation details belong in the authenticated founder panel.

FIRST RULE — Do not panic or rush irreversible actions.
Record the time, preserve evidence, verify the signal through an independent source, and use a trusted clean device for account-security steps. Never share a seed phrase, private key, password or one-time code with anyone claiming to provide support.

01 · Suspected wallet or account compromise

Recognise: unfamiliar sign-ins, unexpected transactions, changed recovery settings, unknown approvals or loss of access.

Respond: use a clean, trusted device; secure the email account and hosting/domain accounts first; change passwords and revoke unknown sessions where the provider supports it. If a wallet key may be exposed, treat that wallet as compromised and seek trusted, independent help to protect remaining assets. Do not enter the recovery phrase into a website or send it to anyone.

Record: transaction signatures, timestamps, affected public addresses and screenshots/logs. Never publish private credentials.

02 · Unexpected minting, transfers or sales

Recognise: supply changes, unfamiliar treasury or founder-wallet transfers, unusual token movements, liquidity withdrawals or abnormal trading.

Respond: verify the mint, token accounts and transaction on an independent Solana explorer/RPC source. Compare against the approved wallet register and known scheduled activity. Escalate confirmed anomalies to RED ALERT. Do not assume a token transfer can be reversed.

Record: transaction signature, slot/time, source and destination accounts, token mint, amount and independent verification links.

03 · Program, authority or vesting change

Recognise: an unexpected program deployment, ProgramData change, upgrade-authority change, vesting schedule alteration, cancellation or withdrawal.

Respond: verify the program ID, executable/deployment data, authority and multisig evidence independently. Pause planned project operations while the event is investigated. Do not attempt an unreviewed upgrade, authority transfer or emergency transaction.

Record: program ID, ProgramData address, deployment slot, authority evidence, stream/account identifiers and relevant signatures.

04 · Website, dashboard or email outage

Recognise: dashboard unavailable, stale timestamps, failed RPC requests, missing email alerts, DNS/hosting errors or unexpected site changes.

Respond: treat monitoring as unavailable, not as a clean security result. Check service-provider status pages from a separate connection; preserve logs; restore from a known-good backup after verifying its integrity. Keep an independent monitoring route and contact method.

Record: outage start/end, affected services, last successful scan, provider incident reference and recovery checks.

05 · Suspected phishing or malicious message

Recognise: urgent requests to connect a wallet, sign a message, install software, reveal credentials or move funds.

Respond: do not click, install, connect or sign. Navigate to known official sites manually. Verify requests using a separate, previously trusted contact channel. Report and preserve the message for investigation.

Record: sender, URL/domain, message headers where available and time received. Do not forward malicious links as clickable public content.

06 · Suspected data breach or administrator access

Recognise: unfamiliar administrator activity, unexpected configuration changes, exposed credentials or access from unknown sessions.

Respond: restrict affected accounts through the hosting/provider's official controls, revoke unknown sessions and rotate affected credentials from a clean device. Preserve audit logs before cleanup. Review access permissions and restore only verified configurations.

Record: account, event time, session/audit evidence, configuration changes and remediation performed.

Incident workflow

Every incident follows the same six stages

1 · Detect & classify

Mark as suspected or confirmed. Assign RED ALERT for credible evidence of compromise, unauthorized authority changes, unexpected minting or critical asset movement.

2 · Alert & preserve

Notify the founder through the configured independent channel. Preserve timestamps, logs, transaction signatures and source links before making changes.

3 · Verify independently

Cross-check the signal with a second trusted source. Check monitoring health and data freshness so outages are not mistaken for normal activity.

4 · Contain safely

Use provider-supported account/session controls and approved incident procedures. No automatic token transfers, signing, authority changes or destructive actions.

5 · Recover & validate

Restore from verified backups, secure affected accounts, validate balances and permissions, and confirm monitoring has resumed with fresh data.

6 · Report & learn

Publish a factual incident summary when safe, record root cause and remediation, and update safeguards and test cases.

Monitoring failure is itself an alert.
Every monitor must report its last successful scan, data source and health. If a scan is late, an RPC source fails, or email delivery cannot be confirmed, ZETHOSPHERE must show “MONITORING DEGRADED” or “MONITORING OFFLINE” — never green or “secure”. Email alerts need an independent delivery test and a secondary notification route.
Founder-only checklist · implementation target

First actions when a RED ALERT arrives

  1. Read the alert evidence and timestamp; confirm whether the monitor itself is healthy.
  2. Open the transaction/program/account evidence using a trusted explorer or provider site entered manually.
  3. Check the affected public address and event against the approved ZETHO address register.
  4. Secure potentially affected email, hosting and administrator accounts from a clean device; revoke unknown sessions where supported.
  5. Do not share credentials, connect a wallet, sign a message or run commands supplied by an unsolicited contact.
  6. Preserve logs and transaction signatures; contact the relevant service provider or a trusted Solana security professional if compromise is credible.
  7. Only resume normal operations after independent verification and a recorded recovery check.
Important limitations: This playbook is defensive guidance, not a guarantee that attacks can be prevented. Solana transactions that are finalized are generally irreversible. A dashboard cannot freeze a wallet or stop every exploit. Any containment action must be technically supported, independently verified and deliberately approved by the founder.
