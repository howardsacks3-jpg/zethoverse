South African legislation: Where South African law applies, relevant legislation may include the Copyright Act 98 of 1978, including sections 2, 23, 24 and 27; the Trade Marks Act 194 of 1993, including sections 33 and 34; and the Counterfeit Goods Act 37 of 1997, including sections 2 and 19. These provisions address, among other matters, copyright subsistence and infringement, civil remedies, certain knowing commercial dealings in infringing copies, registered trade mark infringement, and dealings in counterfeit goods.

Under section 27(6) of the Copyright Act and section 19(1) of the Counterfeit Goods Act, the statutory maximums include fines and imprisonment, with higher maximums for subsequent convictions; the applicable provision, number of articles, facts and current law determine the penalty. These are statutory maxima, not automatic outcomes.

Illustrative South African case law: In Golden China TV Game Centre and Others v Nintendo Co Ltd [1996] ZASCA 103; 1997 (1) SA 405 (A), the Appellate Division considered copyright protection and dealings in unauthorised copies of video games. In Laugh It Off Promotions CC v South African Breweries International (Finance) BV t/a Sabmark International [2005] ZACC 7; 2006 (1) SA 144 (CC), the Constitutional Court considered registered trade mark protection alongside freedom of expression and emphasised that the statutory test must be applied to the facts.
