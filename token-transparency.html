2. Founder vesting schedule

The current selected working schedule divides the 1.5 billion founder allocation into a normal vesting portion of 1.35 billion ZETHO (90%) and a separately restricted exceptional-release allowance of 150 million ZETHO (10%).

The first instalment is due one calendar month after launch, followed by 35 further monthly instalments. The intended mechanism is an automatic on-chain transfer to the published founder destination wallet when an instalment becomes due. The destination wallet and exact schedule must be published before launch.

How scheduled releases will be executed

  • The normal vesting pool is to be held in a dedicated on-chain vesting vault controlled by the vesting program, not by an unrestricted creator withdrawal key.
  • A scheduled execution service (a keeper) submits the transaction when an instalment is due. Solana programs do not wake themselves up; a transaction must be submitted to the network. The keeper pays the transaction fee.
  • The program must independently check the due date, permitted amount, destination and whether that instalment has already been paid.
  • Each instalment may be paid only once. Duplicate keeper submissions must not create duplicate transfers.
  • If an execution is missed or delayed, the overdue instalment remains due. The next successful execution must catch up the overdue instalment rather than silently skip it. Overdue instalments should be processed individually under the published rules, not turned into an unrestricted discretionary withdrawal.
  • A monitored primary keeper and an independent backup execution path should be arranged. The program must remain the authority for whether a release is valid.
  • Before mainnet launch, the program must be tested and independently security-reviewed. A public dashboard is a display of blockchain data; it is not a substitute for the on-chain record.
Reliability commitment: Releases must be administered diligently, timely, reliably and consistently. Negligent failure by the creator or designated operator to carry out the published schedule, without a valid and publicly explained reason, is a breach of ZETHOVERSE's published project rules. Whether conduct also constitutes a breach of law or regulatory obligation depends on the applicable law and facts.

3. Exceptional early-release allowance

A maximum of 150,000,000 ZETHO — 10% of the founder allocation — is being kept separate from the normal monthly vesting pool as a possible exceptional early-release allowance. It is not an automatic payment, an unrestricted founder withdrawal right, or permission to release tokens whenever the market price rises.

  • A price peak by itself is not proof of sustainable demand.
  • Any qualifying release must depend on clear market-health criteria, observation periods and a transparent decision process published before launch.
  • The amount released under this provision must never exceed the published cumulative cap.
  • Every exceptional release must be publicly identified with its amount, date, reason and on-chain transaction reference.
  • Any unused portion remains locked. It does not automatically become available to the founder after the 36 monthly instalments have finished.

The detailed qualifying tests, authorisation method and technical implementation for exceptional releases remain to be finalised. Until those rules are published and implemented, no exceptional release should be treated as available or authorised.

4. Public release records — how everyone can verify

The public must be able to see what was scheduled, what was actually released, when it happened, how much moved and where it went. Transparency must be built into the on-chain design and reflected on this permanent website page; it must not depend only on announcements or a manually maintained spreadsheet.

Once the token and vesting program are deployed, this page should display or link to live, independently verifiable blockchain data, including:

  • Total token supply and each allocation wallet or vault.
  • The founder vesting vault address and the published founder destination wallet.
  • The original launch date and full monthly release calendar.
  • Each instalment's amount, due date and status: scheduled, overdue or completed.
  • Total founder tokens released to date and the remaining locked balance.
  • Every exceptional release, including amount, date and publicly stated reason.
  • Transaction signatures and direct links to the relevant Solscan records.
  • Any approved amendment, the date it was announced, the reason and the applicable effective date.

5. Changes, delays and accountability

No undisclosed or silent change may be made to the published supply, allocation, vesting schedule, release amount, destination or release rules. Any change that is legally and technically permitted must be publicly disclosed in a timely manner, with a clear explanation, the reason for the change, its effect and the date it takes effect. Where feasible, advance public notice and a timelock should apply.

The creator and any appointed operator are expected to administer the published rules diligently, reliably and consistently. A delay must not be concealed. If a release cannot be executed on time, the reason and corrective action must be disclosed publicly, and the overdue instalment must remain visible until resolved.

The intended design is to minimise discretionary control over locked tokens. Any final program should be reviewed for whether its rules can be changed, who can authorise a change, and whether the creator can bypass the restrictions. Those powers and limitations must be disclosed before launch.

6. Launch price and initial liquidity

The launch price has not been selected. The 5 billion token supply does not by itself determine the market price. If ZETHO launches through a decentralised liquidity pool, the initial quoted price will be determined by the ratio of ZETHO tokens to the paired asset (for example, SOL or USDC) placed into that pool.

Initial pool price = value of paired assets ÷ number of ZETHO tokens in the pool
